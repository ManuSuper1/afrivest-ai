The product overview

Project Summary



AfriVest Intelligence is an AI-powered cross-border investment research platform for diasporans, foreign investors, founders, and local entrepreneurs who want to enter any African market without guessing their way into expensive mistakes. The product turns a simple question like “Should I launch this fintech in Ghana?” into a structured market-entry dossier with regulation checks, competitor benchmarking, currency-risk analysis, and a clear go/no-go recommendation.



Built on LangChain’s Deep Agents, the system is designed for the kind of work a normal chatbot fumbles: long, multi-step, research-heavy tasks. Deep Agents is an open-source agent harness built for long-running workflows, with built-in planning, subagents, filesystem tools, and pluggable backends for storing and editing large working files. That makes it a strong fit for a product that needs to keep evidence, compare sources, and evolve a report over time instead of dumping a one-shot answer.



The workflow is simple but powerful: the user selects a country, sector, and investment goal; the agent breaks the research into parallel tracks; specialized subagents handle legal, financial, and market-specific work; and the main agent assembles the findings into a living investment brief saved in a virtual filesystem. LangChain documents subagents as a way to keep the main context clean while delegating specialized work, and the filesystem tools are meant for storing and editing large context without blowing up the conversation window.



Why this is stronger than “just use ChatGPT”



ChatGPT can answer questions. This product builds an investable decision engine. It gives users repeatable due diligence, country-by-country comparisons, saved research artifacts, and a workflow that can be reused for multiple African markets. That makes it more valuable for serious investors than a generic chat experience, because the output is not just advice — it is a structured, traceable investment brief.



Value proposition



For diasporans, it reduces the fear of entering unfamiliar markets. For local founders, it shortens the time between idea and execution. For foreign investors, it provides a faster way to compare countries, understand regulatory friction, and spot opportunity before committing capital.



Positioning line:

“AfriVest Intelligence is the AI due-diligence layer for investing in Africa — built with Deep Agents to turn fragmented market research into clear, actionable investment decisions.”

This gap creates the need for a system that can guide users through the entire investment research process in a structured and reliable way.

CHAPTER TWO: LITERATURE REVIEW AND RELATED WORK
2.1 Introduction
This chapter reviews existing literature and technologies relevant to cross-border investment research, market entry strategies, and the application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in financial decision-making. It explores the traditional methods used for market analysis in Africa, evaluates the limitations of conventional Large Language Models (LLMs) in multi-step research, and examines the emergence of autonomous AI agents. Finally, it reviews related systems to highlight the existing gaps that AfriVest Intelligence aims to fill.

2.2 The Landscape of African Market Entry and Investment
Africa is increasingly recognized as a frontier for high-growth investments, driven by rapid urbanization, a burgeoning youth population, and widespread digital adoption. Sectors such as fintech, health-tech, and renewable energy have seen significant Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) and diasporan interest.

However, cross-border market entry in Africa is notoriously complex. Research highlights several persistent barriers:

Regulatory Fragmentation: Each of the 54 African nations possesses distinct regulatory bodies, compliance requirements, and legal frameworks.

Data Deficits: Reliable, up-to-date market data is often unstructured, paywalled, or scattered across disparate local sources.

Currency and Macroeconomic Risks: High volatility in local currencies requires investors to conduct rigorous, continuous financial risk assessments.

Traditionally, navigating these barriers required hiring specialized on-the-ground consultants or relying on exhaustive, manual desk research—methods that are often prohibitively expensive and time-consuming for diasporans, local founders, and early-stage foreign investors.

2.3 Traditional Investment Research vs. Modern Approaches
Historically, investment due diligence has been the domain of multinational consulting firms and specialized financial analysts. While these traditional approaches yield high-accuracy, comprehensive dossiers, they suffer from significant scalability issues.

Modern digital approaches have attempted to democratize this data through platforms like Bloomberg Terminal or PitchBook. However, these tools are highly quantitative and rely on the user to synthesize the data into a strategic decision. They do not answer qualitative, complex questions such as, "Should I launch this fintech in Ghana?" Therefore, a gap remains between raw data provision and actionable, synthesized market-entry intelligence.

2.4 The Evolution of AI in Financial Research
The integration of Artificial Intelligence into financial services has evolved through several distinct phases:

Quantitative AI: Early applications focused on predictive modeling, algorithmic trading, and credit scoring using structured numerical data.

Robo-Advisors: Systems designed to automate portfolio management based on predefined risk tolerances.

Generative AI and LLMs: The recent explosion of models capable of understanding natural language, summarizing reports, and generating text.

While Generative AI has dramatically reduced the time required to read and summarize financial reports, utilizing these tools for comprehensive due diligence presents unique technical challenges.

2.5 The Limitations of Standard LLMs (e.g., ChatGPT) in Due Diligence
Standard chatbot interfaces powered by LLMs (such as ChatGPT) are highly effective at answering single queries but struggle with the demands of structured investment research. The literature identifies several critical limitations:

Context Window Constraints: Standard chatbots "forget" earlier parts of a conversation when handling massive amounts of data (e.g., reading multiple legal frameworks and competitor reports).

The "One-Shot" Dilemma: Chatbots are designed to generate a single response to a prompt. They do not naturally pause, formulate a research plan, conduct parallel investigations, and iteratively refine a document.

Lack of Traceability: Serious investors require evidence and source comparisons. Standard LLMs often fail to cite sources accurately or structure their outputs into repeatable, standardized formats.

2.6 Autonomous AI Agents and Multi-Agent Frameworks
To overcome the limitations of standard LLMs, the AI industry has shifted toward Agentic Workflows. Autonomous agents are AI systems empowered with tools (such as web search, calculators, and file systems) and the ability to plan and execute multi-step tasks.

LangChain and Deep Agents
LangChain has emerged as a leading framework for developing these advanced workflows. Specifically, the "Deep Agents" architecture allows for the creation of long-running, autonomous research systems. This architecture introduces several paradigm-shifting capabilities:

Subagent Delegation: A primary agent can break down a complex query into parallel tracks, delegating specific tasks (e.g., regulatory checks, financial risk analysis) to specialized subagents. This keeps the main agent’s context window clean and focused on synthesis.

Filesystem Tools: Instead of relying on conversational memory, agents can write, edit, and read from a virtual filesystem. This allows the AI to slowly build a massive, structured document over time without crashing.

Iterative Refinement: Agents can review their own work, compare sources, and adjust their output before presenting the final dossier to the user.

2.7 Review of Related Systems
To contextualize AfriVest Intelligence, it is necessary to compare it against existing solutions used for market research.

2.8 Summary and Gap in the Literature
The review of existing literature and systems reveals a clear gap. While traditional consultants provide the necessary depth and structure for cross-border investment, they are inaccessible to many. Conversely, while modern AI chatbots are accessible and fast, they lack the multi-step reasoning, context retention, and structural rigor required for serious due diligence.

There is currently a lack of platforms specifically engineered to combine the rigor of traditional market-entry dossiers with the speed of AI, tailored exclusively for the African market. AfriVest Intelligence addresses this gap by utilizing a multi-agent framework (Deep Agents) to transform fragmented data into structured, actionable investment decisions, thereby democratizing access to high-level market entry intelligence.

### 3.2 System Architecture and Design Philosophy

AfriVest Intelligence is built on a decoupled, modular client-server architecture designed to handle long-running, asynchronous, and research-intensive workflows without degrading user experience.

The architecture consists of three primary layers:

1. **Presentation Layer (Frontend):** A responsive web interface built with **Next.js** and styled with **Tailwind CSS**, designed from high-fidelity prototypes created in **Figma**.
2. **Application & Orchestration Layer (API Backend & AI Harness):** A high-performance **FastAPI** backend that manages API routing, session management, and wraps the **LangChain Deep Agents** engine.
3. **Execution & Artifact Layer (Agent Subsystems & Virtual Filesystem):** Autonomous subagents executing parallelized research tasks and persisting intermediate findings to an isolated virtual filesystem.

### 3.3 Multi-Agent Research Engine (LangChain Deep Agents)

Standard Large Language Models fail in long-running research due to context window saturation and token drift. To resolve this, AfriVest Intelligence utilizes LangChain’s Deep Agents architecture, which integrates **hierarchical subagent delegation**, **explicit planning steps**, and a **virtual filesystem**.

#### 3.3.1 Architectural Components

* **Lead Orchestrator Agent:** Receives the user’s target country, industry, and investment goal. It develops a multi-phase research plan, partitions the scope into distinct tracks, spawns domain-specific subagents, and synthesizes final outputs into a structured investment brief.
* **Specialized Subagents:**
* **Regulatory & Compliance Subagent:** Identifies local licensing requirements, foreign ownership caps, data residency laws, and regulatory oversight bodies (e.g., Bank of Ghana, Central Bank of Nigeria).
* **Competitor Benchmarking Subagent:** Maps market incumbents, direct/indirect competitors, market share estimates, and structural moats.
* **Financial & Macroeconomic Risk Subagent:** Analyzes foreign exchange (FX) volatility, inflation trends, capital repatriation constraints, and local tax exposure.


* **Virtual Filesystem (VFS):** Subagents write raw findings and citations into working markdown files in an isolated environment. This keeps the primary agent's context window clean while preserving source traceability.

The end-to-end execution follows a structured multi-phase pipeline:

### 3.5 Target Test Industries & Validation Case Studies

To rigorously test the system against real-world complexities, the platform will be evaluated using two representative, high-friction sectors in Sub-Saharan Africa:

#### 1. Case Study 1: FinTech in Ghana

* **Context:** Launching a Cross-Border Payment Service Provider (PSP) / Remittance Solution in Ghana.
* **Key Research Checkpoints:**
* Regulatory compliance with the **Bank of Ghana (Payment Systems and Services Act, 2019 - Act 987)**.
* Minimum capital requirements for Tier-1, Tier-2, or Tier-3 licenses.
* Competitor benchmarking against local players (e.g., Hubtel, Zeepay, ExpressPay).
* Macroeconomic evaluation of Ghanaian Cedi (GHS) depreciation and foreign currency account regulations.



#### 2. Case Study 2: CleanTech / Commercial Solar in Kenya

* **Context:** Deploying Commercial & Industrial (C&I) Solar Systems for manufacturing plants in Kenya.
* **Key Research Checkpoints:**
* Regulatory approvals from the **Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA)**.
* Import tariff exemptions and VAT incentives on solar components under Kenyan tax law.
* Competitor analysis (e.g., M-KOPA, Sun King, D.light).
* FX risk modeling regarding dollar-denominated power purchase agreements (PPAs) versus Kenyan Shilling (KES) collections.

### 3.6 Quality Assurance (QA) and Evaluation Framework

To confirm system accuracy, robustness, and stability, a dual evaluation strategy combining functional QA test cases and qualitative output evaluation metrics is established.

#### 3.6.1 Functional QA Test Cases

The system undergoes unit, integration, and performance testing across several operational parameters:

| Test ID | Test Category | Objective | Expected Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **TC-01** | Input Validation | Submit incomplete or malformed sector/country inputs. | System rejects request with a 422 Unprocessable Entity and returns actionable error prompts. |
| **TC-02** | Parallel Agent Spawning | Verify parallel task handoff from Lead Agent to Subagents. | Subagents execute concurrently without deadlocks or thread blocking. |
| **TC-03** | Virtual Filesystem I/O | Verify subagents write, read, and append to designated markdown files. | Files are created, isolated per session, and retrieved without cross-tenant data leaks. |
| **TC-04** | API Latency & Streaming | Measure response times and streaming stability over FastAPI endpoints. | API streams status progress updates reliably within acceptable latency bounds. |
| **TC-05** | Go/No-Go Decision Logic | Evaluate contradictory inputs (e.g., low capital vs. high regulatory barrier). | System issues a conditional or "No-Go" flag with explicit risk citations. |

#### 3.6.2 Qualitative & AI Evaluation Metrics

In addition to functional testing, the quality of generated dossiers is evaluated against three core domain criteria:

1. **Factual Faithfulness & Citation Accuracy:** The proportion of regulatory claims and competitor facts that match official source documents without hallucinations.
2. **Report Completeness:** Ensuring all mandatory sections (Legal, Competitors, Currency/FX, Recommendation) are fully articulated without placeholder text.
3. **Traceability:** Ensuring every claim in the final synthesis references a verifiable file artifact generated in the virtual filesystem.
